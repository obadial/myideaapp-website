Practice investing without real money: StockWatch, Investopedia or a broker demo?

By MyIdeApp, the studio behind StockWatch. Comparison based on each app's official pages. Read in FR · ES

The short answer

StockWatch is an educational iPhone app: you build a virtual portfolio with your own trades, follow live prices and compare your performance with the S&P 500, with no account and never connected to a bank. Investopedia's free web simulator gives you $100,000 in virtual cash and lets you play with friends. A broker demo account mirrors its real interface but means signing up with that broker.

Why choose StockWatch

  • No link to a bank or broker: you can't place a real order by mistake.
  • Your portfolio, watchlist and alerts stay on your iPhone.
  • Free: 10 tracked stocks, 1 portfolio, 3 alerts, sector heatmap, DCA simulator.
  • Premium ($3.99/month or $24.99/year) unlocks unlimited use and multiple portfolios.

When another app is a better fit

For a class or friends competition with the same starting capital, Investopedia's simulator is built for it. If you've already chosen a broker and want to learn its interface before investing for real, its demo account is the most realistic. StockWatch gives no financial advice and doesn't replace a professional.

Frequently asked questions

Does StockWatch place real trades?

No. It is an educational tool, never connected to a bank or broker.

Are prices real-time?

StockWatch shows live prices from public sources; a slight delay is possible depending on the exchange.
